# Phase 1.4: Signal Detection - Design (short)

**Intent:** find statistically unusual increases in hiring for a company × role family ×
country, against that cell's own rolling baseline. An example: "Stripe opened 3× its usual
Solutions Engineering roles in Ireland in the last 4 weeks." That is the product's core value:
earlier visibility of expansion than official announcements give.

**Brief:** "Monitoring localized ATS hiring anomalies to expose hidden growth"; rolling baseline +
z-score per company / role / location.

## Findings from the real data (local DB, 2026-11-01)
- **No observed history yet:** 1 scrape run. A baseline over our own observations needs ≥ 8
  weekly points, which means ~8 weeks of scheduled scraping.
- **`first_published` exists for all 2,322 postings, but only for postings that are still open.**
  Monthly counts rise steeply toward today for every company (Anthropic 3 → 184, Databricks
  6 → 199, Stripe 5 → 240). That rise is **survivorship bias** (old postings have closed), not
  growth. Absolute z-scores on this history would flag every company as expanding.
- **Cells are sparse.** Over the last ~26 weeks there are 293 company × family × country cells;
  12 have ≥ 26 postings, 48 have ≥ 10, and 148 have only 1–2. Plain z-scores on such counts
  divide by ~0.

## Approaches
1. **Two detectors, each used where it is valid (chosen, assistant's call).**
   - **`volume` (primary, the brief's detector).** Weekly *observed new openings* per cell. A
     posting is a new opening when it is first seen after the company's first successful
     snapshot. Each week is compared with a rolling baseline of the previous 8 complete weeks,
     using a count-aware z-score. It gives no signals until history exists; it reports
     `insufficient_history` instead.
   - **`mix_shift` (bootstrap, provisional).** Per company: the share of a cell in the last 4
     weeks of `first_published` openings, against its share in the 12 weeks before, using a
     two-proportion z-test. Survivorship bias mostly **cancels in a share**, because it inflates
     every cell of a company. Residual bias remains for cells with faster or slower fill times
     (e.g. sales vs staff engineering), so these signals are labelled `provisional`.
2. Volume z-score on `first_published` history. Rejected: the survivorship ramp makes every
   cell look anomalous.
3. Stock metric (week-over-week change in open postings from `snapshot_postings`). Valid, but
   it also needs history, and it mixes hiring with filling. Deferred; the same machinery
   applies later.

## Statistics (pinned)
- **Weeks** are ISO weeks starting Monday 00:00 UTC. Only **complete** weeks before `as_of` are
  scored.
- **Volume:** `x` = new openings in week `w`.
  - Baseline: the previous `B = 8` weeks that have data. A week counts only if the company had
    ≥ 1 **`ok`** snapshot that week. Outage weeks and partial-only weeks are *missing*, not
    zero. A partial fetch under-counts new openings and would mimic a freeze. *(User change
    2026-11-01, needed for contraction signals.)*
  - `μ` = mean and `s` = sample std of the baseline.
  - `σ_eff = sqrt(max(s², μ, 1))`. This is a Poisson floor: sparse counts can never produce an
    infinite z.
  - `z = (x − μ) / σ_eff`.
  - **Expansion signal** when `z ≥ 3.0` **and** `x ≥ 5` **and** baseline has `B` weeks.
  - **Contraction signal** (hiring freeze / restructuring) when `z ≤ −3.0` **and** `μ ≥ 5` **and**
    the baseline has `B` weeks.
    - With the Poisson floor, `z_min = −√μ`, so a contraction is impossible unless `μ ≥ 9`.
      Tiny cells can never fake a freeze.
- **Mix shift:** within a company, `n_r`, `N_r` are the cell's and company's openings in the
  recent `R = 4` weeks; `n_b`, `N_b` are the same for the `12` weeks before.
  - `p = (n_r + n_b) / (N_r + N_b)`.
  - `z = (n_r/N_r − n_b/N_b) / sqrt(p(1−p)(1/N_r + 1/N_b))`.
  - **Expansion** when `z ≥ 3.0` **and** `n_r ≥ 5` **and** `N_b ≥ 30`.
  - **Contraction** when `z ≤ −3.0` **and** `n_b ≥ 5` **and** `N_b ≥ 30`.
  - Both directions are `provisional`. Slow-filling roles are over-represented in the older,
    survivorship-biased baseline window, which biases their share *down* recently.
- **Direction and severity:**
  - `direction` is `expansion` or `contraction`.
  - `|z| ≥ 4` is `strong`; `3 ≤ |z| < 4` is `notable`.
  - The **raw z, observed and baseline values are always stored**, so the dashboard can apply
    its own colour bands (e.g. yellow at |z| = 3, red at |z| > 5). *(User change 2026-11-01.)*
- **Cell levels:** `(company)`, `(company, family)`, `(company, country)` and
  `(company, family, country)`.
  - Country comes from a posting's canonical locations: their `country_code`, or `ZZ` when the
    posting has none.
  - A posting counts **once per cell** (`COUNT DISTINCT posting_id`) even with several
    locations in that country.

## Storage
- `signal_runs`: `id`, `computed_at`, `as_of`, `signals_version`, `params` (JSON text).
- `signal_scores`: one row per run × detector × level × cell × scored week. Columns:
  - `observed`, `baseline_mean`, `baseline_std`, `z`, `baseline_weeks`;
  - `status`: `scored | insufficient_history | below_min_count`;
  - `is_signal`, `direction` (`expansion | contraction | NULL`), `severity`,
    `basis` (`observed | published`).
  - The raw `z` and raw values are stored for **every** scored row, signal or not.
- Every row has the run's `signals_version` and params, so a score is always reproducible.

## Decisions (assistant's calls - flag any to change)
- **pandas** is added for weekly aggregation. It is the brief's stack, and numpy is already
  present via spaCy.
- **Seniority** is not a cell dimension yet (too sparse); it can be added as a level later.
- **City level is skipped** (too sparse); country is the location grain.
- **Invocation:** a `detect` command (`scripts/run_detect.py [--as-of DATE] [--db URL]`)
  writes a new run each time; old runs are kept for audit. The pipeline order is scrape →
  harmonize → detect.
- **Thresholds** (`B=8`, `R=4`, `12`, `z≥3`, minimum counts) live in one `DetectorParams`
  dataclass with these defaults. The dashboard reads the run's stored params.
